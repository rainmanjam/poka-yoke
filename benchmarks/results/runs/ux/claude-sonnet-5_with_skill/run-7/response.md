Not quite — a confirm modal is the weakest device on the ladder for this. Users learn to reflex-click through "Are you sure?" dialogs (that's the click-through blindness that's presumably how they're deleting workspaces accidentally in the first place), so it adds friction without actually preventing much.

The stronger fix: make deletion **reversible** — soft-delete into a trash with a retention window (e.g. 30 days) plus a "Deleted. Undo" toast on the action itself. No friction on the happy path, full recovery on the accidental one, and support tickets stop being panicked because there's something to point people to. If you still want a gate on top for the *permanent* purge, that's the one place a modal (or better, type-the-workspace-name-to-confirm) earns its keep — GitHub's repo-deletion pattern.
